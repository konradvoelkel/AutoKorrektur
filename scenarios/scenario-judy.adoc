= Scenario: Judy
:persona: Judy
:tool-type: web+android
:run: 002
:kind: new-ground

== Persona

Judy is a 31-year-old care worker in Vienna who grew up speaking Turkish and uses her phone in Turkish.
Context / device: phone set to Turkish (tr-TR); she also opens the website on the same phone. Her German is conversational but she does not read German comfortably, and her English is worse than her German.
Expertise: intermediate with phones. She has never sideloaded an app and does not read release notes.
Mindset: confused rather than hostile, and prone to assuming an app is "not for me" when it speaks a language she does not read. She will look for a language switch before she looks for anything else.

== Goal

Judy wants to work out whether this app is usable for her at all in a language she can read, and if so get as far as a car-free picture of her street.

== Starting point

She opens https://autokorrektur.org/ on a Turkish-locale phone, then (if she gets that far) the installed app with its language left at the system default.

== Success criteria

Judy can say which language the site and the app speak to her, whether she found a way to change it, and how far she got. Any place where the app or the site mixes two languages in one screen is recorded.

== Environment note

There is no `values-tr` and no Turkish page: the app falls back to `values/` (English) and
the site to whatever its content negotiation or default does. Neither is a defect in itself.
What is under test is whether a speaker of a third language can find the language they can
read, and whether the fallback is clean or mixed. The app's per-app language override must
be left at system default for this run so the real fallback is what Judy meets.
