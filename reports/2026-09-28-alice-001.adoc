== Context

Tool: https://autokorrektur.org/, German language, mobile viewport 375x812 (Android phone simulation).
Goal (from scenario): Get AutoKorrektur onto her own phone right now, starting from the website, so she can show car-free renderings of her street at a district council meeting in 40 minutes.
How run: Browser tools with mobile phone viewport emulation.

== Walkthrough

=== Step 1: Landing page, initial impression

Scan::
Alice sees the German homepage with:
- Logo and title "AutoKorrektur" at the top
- Navigation: "Download", "Datenschutz", "English"
- Large heading in German: "Deine Straße ohne Autos. Berechnet auf deinem Gerät." (Your street without cars. Computed on your device.)
- Description paragraph: "AutoKorrektur erkennt parkende Autos in deinem Foto und lässt sie verschwinden – die KI läuft komplett auf dem Smartphone. Kein Konto, kein Upload, kein Tracking. Ein Vorher/Nachher-Bild zum Teilen in zwei Tipps." (recognizes parked cars, AI runs entirely on phone, no account/upload/tracking, before/after image to share in two taps)
- Two buttons visible:
  * A prominent brown button: "Für Android herunterladen" with text "APK · 122 MB · Version 2.0.0-15-ga7f59c5"
  * Below it, a secondary button: "Installieren & prüfen" with subtitle "Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat"
- A before/after image of a street

Expect::
Alice sees what looks like a straightforward way to get the app. The main brown button is labeled "Download for Android," which sounds like the standard path. She doesn't understand the word "APK"—it is jargon to her. The second button's subtitle with "Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat" (technical terms: sideloading, checksum, certificate) sounds confusing and intimidating, not the normal way to install an app. She will tap the main brown button, expecting either a direct download to her phone or possibly a redirect to Google Play Store.

Act::
Tap the prominent "Für Android herunterladen" (Download for Android) button.

Observe::
The button is a clickable link that points to `download/autokorrektur-2.0.0-15-ga7f59c5-arm64-v8a.apk`, a direct APK file download of 122 MB. On a real Android phone, tapping this would initiate downloading the APK file to the device's Downloads folder. The browser interaction timed out, consistent with a large file download starting.

Verdict::
EXPECTED in the moment of tapping (she tapped what appeared to be a download button), but SURPRISING in outcome — severity **Critical**

Note::
Alice has now triggered a download of a file she doesn't understand. Three immediate problems:
1. **Alice does not know what "APK" is.** The prominent interface labels the file as "APK" but never explains what this means or why it is different from installing an app from Google Play. A non-technical person might think "APK" is a brand name or format, not an installable app file.
2. **No explanation of what to do next.** After the download completes, Alice will have a file in her Downloads folder, but the landing page does not explain (in the visible area) what happens next or how to install it. She may think the download itself is the installation, or she may be confused and give up.
3. **Installation instructions are hidden below the fold.** The page contains a section "Android: herunterladen und installieren" (Android: download and install) with clear step-by-step instructions, but they are not visible on the landing view. A rushed person at a bus stop with 40 minutes to spare may not scroll to find them.

The second button ("Installieren & prüfen") links to that section (#android anchor), but Alice dismissed it as confusing jargon and never clicked it.

=== Step 2: What Alice would find if she scrolled (discovery via page structure analysis)

Scan (theoretical)::
If Alice scrolls down, she would see:
- Detailed section "Android: herunterladen und installieren" (Android: download and install)
- A table listing the APK file, its target, and size (122 MB)
- Installation instructions (translated): "Install ('Sideloading'): Download the file to your phone, tap it in the notification or in the Downloads folder and allow the browser to install unknown apps when Android asks. Android checks the rest itself."
- Version and certificate information
- A warning: "Dies ist ein früher Testbuild" (This is an early test build)

Expect (if she scrolled)::
Alice would find step-by-step instructions, written in a more conversational tone. She would learn:
- Tap the downloaded file from Downloads or the notification
- Android will ask permission to install unknown apps
- She should grant permission
- Then the app installs

But she likely does not scroll, because:
- She is rushed (40 minutes to meeting)
- She is standing, holding a phone one-handed
- The prominent button already triggered the download
- She expects the UI to guide her next steps if there are any

Observe (actual discovery)::
Page structure analysis reveals the instructions do exist, but below the fold. The second button ("Installieren & prüfen") is meant to be a shortcut to this section, but its subtitle "Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat" is jargon that repels a novice.

Verdict::
SURPRISING — severity **High**. The help is there but invisible to someone in Alice's situation.

Note::
The term "Sideloading" is a blocker. Alice has never sideloaded an APK and may not know what it means. A native German speaker, she would benefit from a clearer label like "Schritt für Schritt Anleitung zum Installieren" (Step by step installation guide) rather than technical jargon. The checksum and certificate discussion further confuses the path: these are security features she does not need to understand to install the app.

== Outcome

Goal achieved::
Partially. Alice has found the download link and triggered the file download to her device. However, she does not understand what an "APK" is, and the next steps (how to install it, what to do when Android prompts about unknown sources) are not visible in the primary flow. She would need to either scroll to find instructions or take a guess. Given her rush and lack of technical knowledge, she is at high risk of:
- Thinking the download is the entire process and trying to open the file without understanding what it is
- Getting stuck when Android prompts about "unknown sources" and not knowing whether to allow it
- Giving up and looking for the app on Google Play instead
- Showing up to her meeting without the app

Time / steps to goal::
2 steps to trigger download (land on page, tap button). Estimated time: 30–60 seconds on mobile data at a bus stop. However, the **actual** goal (having a working, installed app on her phone) would require scrolling to find installation instructions and completing the installation process, which would add 2–3 more minutes, contingent on understanding the Android "unknown sources" prompt.

Where the persona struggled or gave up::
Alice has not yet struggled or given up, but the next critical moment is immediate:
1. After the download completes (seconds to minutes depending on connection speed), she will tap the downloaded file.
2. Android will display a dialog: "Allow installation from this source? You haven't installed apps from '[browser name]' before." (or similar, depending on OS version).
3. If Alice is confident, she taps "Allow" and the app installs.
4. If Alice is uncertain, she may panic ("Is this safe? Is this malware?") and cancel. If she cancels, she has a useless downloaded file and no clear way forward.
5. The landing page has given her no reassurance that this prompt is expected and normal.

== Findings (raw)

- **Critical: "APK" is unexplained jargon.** The main button labels the file as "APK · 122 MB" without defining or contextualizing what APK means. A non-technical German user will not know this is the Android application format. This is a major barrier to confidence. Recommendation: Consider "Installationsdatei" (installation file) or "App-Datei" (app file) as a more intuitive label, or add a tooltip/explanation.

- **Critical: No next-step guidance in the primary flow.** After Alice taps the download button, she is left without instruction. The page does not explain what will happen when the file arrives, what she should do with it, or how to handle Android's security prompts. A rushed person will not scroll to find buried instructions. Recommendation: Add a small note or overlay near the download button: "Die Datei wird auf deinem Telefon heruntergeladen. Tippe sie an, um die Installation zu starten." (The file will download to your phone. Tap it to start the installation.)

- **High: Installation instructions are hidden below the fold.** The step-by-step guide exists but is not visible on first load. On a mobile phone in a rushing situation, Alice will not scroll to find it. The table with the APK file details and the installation explanation are valuable but unreachable by the primary path. Recommendation: Move the installation instructions into a clear section visible on the landing page, or expand the second button's affordance to make it more inviting (e.g., "Installation and Verification — Step by Step" instead of the cryptic "Installieren & prüfen: Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat").

- **High: Technical jargon repels the novice.** The second button reads "Installieren & prüfen" (Install & verify) with subtitle "Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat" (Sideloading, checksum, certificate). Alice does not know these words. "Sideloading" is especially alienating—it implies a workaround, not the normal path. She will pass it by. Recommendation: Rewrite as "Schritt-für-Schritt Anleitung" (Step-by-step guide) or "Sicherheit prüfen" (Verify security) without technical jargon in the label. Reserve technical details for a collapsible "Advanced" section if needed.

- **High: No reassurance about Android's security prompt.** When Android says "Allow installation from unknown sources?", Alice will have no context that this is normal and expected. She may fear the app is unsafe or that she is doing something wrong. Recommendation: Include a brief note on the page (visible above the fold): "Android fragt nach Erlaubnis für 'unbekannte Quellen' — das ist normal und sicher." (Android will ask about unknown sources—this is normal and safe.)

- **Medium: The file is large (122 MB) for mobile data.** Alice is on mobile data at a bus stop. A 122 MB download can take several minutes on a typical mobile network. The page shows the size, which is good, but does not warn about data usage or suggest wifi as an alternative. Recommendation: Add a note for mobile users: "Das ist eine große Datei. Über Mobilfunk kann der Download einige Minuten dauern. WLAN ist schneller." (This is a large file. On mobile data, the download can take several minutes. WiFi is faster.)

- **Medium: "Ein Vorher/Nachher-Bild zum Teilen in zwei Tipps" (before/after image in two taps) sets an expectation Alice may not meet.** The headline promises the result is ready to share in two taps. After download and installation, Alice still needs to take or upload a photo and run the algorithm, which could take seconds to minutes. The two taps are after that work is done. Expectation-setting is off. (Minor issue, as the description paragraph clarifies the full workflow.)

- **Delight: The description is clear and in native German.** The text "Kein Konto, kein Upload, kein Tracking" (no account, no upload, no tracking) directly addresses privacy concerns and is written for German speakers, not machine-translated. The before/after image is persuasive. Alice understands the value proposition immediately. Recommendation: Keep this clarity throughout the installation flow.

- **Delight: The "Download" link in the navigation bar is an alternative entry point.** A user who misses the main button could tap "Download" and be taken to the installation section (#android). This is a good failsafe. Alice didn't use it, but it exists. Recommendation: Ensure this link is prominent and leads directly to clear installation steps.

== Summary

**Goal achieved: Partially.** Alice found the download and initiated it, but she does not understand what an APK is, the installation instructions are hidden from her, and she has no preparation for Android's security prompt. She is at high risk of becoming confused or giving up.

**Three worst problems:**

1. **Critical: "APK" is unexplained.** Alice does not know what to download or why. Recommendation: Replace with a more intuitive label or add a brief explanation.

2. **Critical: No guidance for the next step after download.** The page does not tell Alice what to do when the file arrives or how to handle Android's prompts. She will be lost after tapping download. Recommendation: Add explicit next-step instructions visible on the landing page or in a prompt after the download starts.

3. **High: Installation instructions are below the fold and guarded by confusing jargon.** The second button's label and subtitle ("Sideloading, Prüfsumme, Zertifikat") are technical terms that a novice will not understand or will actively avoid. The full instructions are hidden. Recommendation: Rewrite the button and move or expand the instructions to be visible on the primary flow.
