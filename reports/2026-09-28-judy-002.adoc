== Context

Tool: https://autokorrektur.org/ and the installed app AutoKorrektur, package
`de.konradvoelkel.android.autokorrektur`, version "2.0.0-18-gc739cf6" (seen in the About dialog).

Goal (from scenario): Judy wants to work out whether this app is usable for her at all in a
language she can read, and if so get as far as a car-free picture of her street. She looks for a
language switch before anything else.

How run: Website opened in the Claude Browser at a 375x812 mobile viewport, starting at
`https://autokorrektur.org/`. App launched on `emulator-5556` (Turkish system locale, no
per-app override, freshly cleared data) via
`monkey -p de.konradvoelkel.android.autokorrektur -c android.intent.category.LAUNCHER 1`.
Seeded photos in DCIM/Camera: `IMG_20260927_083104.jpg` (cobblestone street, one parked blue
car) and `IMG_20260927_084512.png` (no cars); the street photo was used per the scenario goal.

== Mixed-language screens

- *About AutoKorrektur dialog* (main menu → About & Licenses): dialog title and full body in
  English ("About AutoKorrektur", the app description, version, license and credits text); the
  single dismiss button reads *"Tamam"* (Turkish "OK") — the Android system's own localized
  string for a standard dialog's positive button, sitting inside an otherwise all-English dialog.
- *Diagnostics dialog* (main menu → Diagnostics): dialog title "Diagnostics", toggle label
  "Record diagnostics on this device", and the full explanatory paragraph are English; of the
  three action buttons, "Delete" and "Export" are English (app-supplied) but the middle button
  reads *"İptal"* (Turkish "Cancel") — again the system's localized neutral-button string.
- *Android system share sheet* (after tapping "Share image"): the sheet's own chrome is Turkish
  ("Resim paylaşılıyor" = "Sharing image", "Paylaşmak için önerilen kullanıcı yok" = "No
  suggested user to share with"), but the row of target apps mixes languages by app: "Yazdır"
  (Turkish, "Print") sits next to "Quick Share", "Drive", "Maps" / "Add to Maps", and "Messages"
  (all left in English or as untranslated brand names). This is OS/other-app behaviour, not
  AutoKorrektur's, but it sits directly inside the task Judy is asked to complete.
- *Toolbar overflow icon content-description*: the visible "⋮" icon carries no on-screen text,
  but its accessibility content-desc is "Diğer seçenekler" (Turkish, "more options") — invisible
  to a sighted user, but worth recording as the first Turkish string the app-adjacent chrome
  produces.

== Outcome

Goal achieved:: Yes — Judy reached a car-free before/after image of the street photo and could
tell the car was gone from the visual alone, without needing to read the English labels
("BEFORE"/"AFTER") accompanying it.

Language she ended up reading:: The app spoke English throughout (no Turkish translation exists,
per the environment note); the surrounding Android system chrome (photo picker, share sheet
chrome, some share-target names, and the OK/Cancel buttons inside the app's own dialogs) spoke
Turkish, because that follows the phone's system locale independently of the app.

Whether she found a language switch:: Site: yes — "English" in the top nav, above the fold on
both the German and English pages, a clean two-way toggle, no third (Turkish) option available.
App: no — the only menu (three-dot overflow → "Diagnostics" / "About & Licenses") contains no
language or settings entry of any kind; per-app language is not something Judy would know to look
for in Android's own system settings, and nothing in the app points her there.

Where the persona struggled or gave up:: She did not give up — the icon-driven main screen
("Take Photo" / "Select Image" with recognisable icons) and the visual nature of the before/after
result let her complete the scenario's goal by recognising shapes and scenes rather than reading
prose. Her friction was entirely in secondary screens: the two AlertDialogs she opened while
hunting for a language setting (About, Diagnostics) turned out to be dead ends that were also
disorienting (English body text, Turkish dismiss button), and two of her taps needed a retry
(About's "Tamam" button, "Select Image") for reasons unrelated to language.

== Findings (raw)

  dialog content.
- Medium — same pattern repeats verbatim in the "Diagnostics" dialog: English title/body/toggle,
  but the neutral button reads "İptal" (Turkish "Cancel") next to English "Delete"/"Export" — this
  is a systemic pattern across every standard AlertDialog in the app, not a one-off.
- Medium — the Android system share sheet itself mixes languages by app: "Yazdır" (Turkish
  "Print") sits beside "Quick Share", "Drive", "Maps", "Messages" left in English/brand form; this
  happens squarely inside the share step of Judy's task even though it isn't the app's own text.
- Low — no in-app language or settings control exists anywhere (only "Diagnostics" and "About &
  Licenses" in the overflow menu); a Turkish-reading user has no way to change the app's language
  from within the app itself.
- Low — two taps needed a retry to register (About dialog's "Tamam" button, and the "Select
  Image" button on the main screen) — not a language issue, but the kind of friction that erodes
  confidence for a user who is already unsure the app is "for her."
- Low (invisible to sighted users) — the toolbar overflow icon's accessibility content-desc,
  "Diğer seçenekler," is Turkish while the icon carries no visible label at all.
- Delight — the system Photo Picker sheet (tabs "Fotoğraflar"/"Albümler", the privacy banner, the
  "Eylül" month header) is fully and cleanly Turkish; for a moment the flow feels like it speaks
  Judy's language, even though this is OS chrome rather than the app's own UI.
- Delight — the core "Take Photo" / "Select Image" main screen, and the final before/after result,
  are both legible by icon and image alone: Judy can complete the entire happy path — pick a
  street photo, see the car disappear — without reading a single word of English, aside from

- Website — the language switch ("English" link) is exactly where Judy looks first: top nav,

  leftovers. No Turkish page exists, which the scenario explicitly treats as expected, not a
  defect — but it means Judy's best available reading language on the site (English) is, per her
  own profile, her weakest of the three.
