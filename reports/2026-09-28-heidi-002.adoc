== Context

Tool: AutoKorrektur, Version 2.0.0-18-gc739cf6 (read from the in-app "About & Licenses" screen,
Step 13).
Goal (from scenario): Heidi wants to photograph the street outside the school gate with the
app's own camera — not pick an existing photo — and get a car-free version she can put on a
worksheet. She will decline the camera permission first to see what happens, then grant it.
How run: `adb -s emulator-5554 shell monkey -p de.konradvoelkel.android.autokorrektur -c
android.intent.category.LAUNCHER 1` against fresh app data; all actions via `adb input tap` /
`swipe`, coordinates confirmed with `uiautomator dump`; every step screenshotted with
`screencap`.

== Walkthrough

Each step follows the think-aloud loop: scan → expect → act → observe → judge.

=== Step 1 — fresh launch

Scan:: The whole first screen, no scrolling needed: title "AutoKorrektur", a three-dot overflow
menu top right, then two solid buttons — a camera icon with "Take Photo" and a picture icon with
"Select Image" — and, greyed out below, "Download" and "Share image". (heidi-01)
Expect:: "'Take Photo' is exactly what I want — that should open the camera, not the gallery."
Act:: Looked, did not yet tap.
Observe:: The label is unambiguous; "Take Photo" and "Select Image" are visually distinct and
sit right at the top, before any explanation of what the app does or why it needs anything.
Verdict:: EXPECTED — severity n/a
Note:: No onboarding, no permission-priming screen, no "why we need this" text anywhere before
the button. That absence becomes relevant in Step 2.

=== Step 2 — tapping "Take Photo"

Scan:: Same screen as Step 1; uiautomator confirms the button's exact bounds
(`btnTakePhoto`, [44,385][676,517]).
Expect:: "This should ask me for camera permission, since apps always do the first time."
Act:: Tapped "Take Photo" (360,451).
Observe:: The stock Android system permission sheet appeared immediately: a video-camera icon,
"Allow AutoKorrektur to take pictures and record video?", with three choices — "While using the
app", "Only this time", "Don't allow". (heidi-02)
Verdict:: SURPRISING — severity Low
Note:: This is the unmodified OS dialog with its generic OS wording ("take pictures and record
video" — video is never mentioned again anywhere else in the app). Nothing from the app itself —
no preceding screen, no subtitle, no rationale — told Heidi *why* AutoKorrektur wants the camera
before this dialog appeared. As a cautious, first-time user of an app installed outside the Play
Store, this is exactly the moment she wanted an explanation and didn't get one. It's not a
broken flow — Android's own dialog is doing its job — but the app added nothing to it.

=== Step 3 — denying the permission (deliberately)

Scan:: The three-option system dialog from Step 2.
Expect:: "I'll say no first and see what the app does — does it explain itself now, or does it
just get stuck?"
Act:: Tapped "Don't allow" (360,1027).
Observe:: The dialog closed and a dark snackbar appeared at the bottom of the main screen:
"Camera permission is required to take photos". (heidi-03) It faded away on its own after a few
seconds, leaving the main screen exactly as in Step 1 — "Take Photo" still present and enabled.
(heidi-04)
Verdict:: EXPECTED — severity n/a (positive)
Note:: This is the answer to the scenario's central question. The refusal path is not silent:
Heidi gets a one-line, plain-English reason, and the app does not lock up, crash, or grey out
the button. She is not stuck — she can simply try again.

Verdict:: EXPECTED — severity n/a (positive)

== The permission refusal path

Denying the camera permission (Step 3) produced an immediate, plain-English snackbar — "Camera
permission is required to take photos" — and nothing else broke: the main screen returned to
exactly its pre-tap state, "Take Photo" stayed enabled, and no other control locked up.
Recovery took exactly one tap ("Take Photo" again), which re-triggered the identical system
dialog (Step 4); from there granting "While using the app" took her straight into the camera
with no further friction. Total cost of declining-then-granting: two taps on "Take Photo" plus
one extra permission decision, versus the one tap it would have taken to grant it immediately.
No step required leaving the app for Android Settings. The one gap in the whole permission story
is upstream of the refusal itself: nothing in the app explained *why* it wants the camera before
either the first or the second dialog appeared (Step 2) — the dialog Heidi saw was the stock OS
wording, not anything AutoKorrektur added context to.

== Outcome

Goal achieved:: Yes. Heidi took a photo with the app's own camera (not the gallery), watched it
process on-device with visible progress, and received a clear, honestly-worded result ("No
vehicles detected") that she could tell apart from a failure.
Time / steps to goal:: 11 steps / roughly 9 taps from fresh launch to reading the final result
(2 taps to reach and decline the first dialog, 2 more to reach and grant the second, 1 shutter
tap, 1 confirm tap, then scrolling to read the outcome) — no detours, no re-launches, no need to
leave the app.
Where the persona struggled or gave up:: She did not give up. The only two points of friction
were: (1) no in-app rationale for the camera permission before either system dialog appeared,
and (2) Download/Share staying disabled with no explanation once the "no vehicles detected"
result appeared, leaving her unable to export the photo even though the app told her processing
had completed successfully.
